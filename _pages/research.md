## Research interests

My research lies at the intersection of law and politics, with a focus on democratic backsliding and public attitudes toward courts. Methodologically, I draw mostly on experimental methods.

## Works in progress

**Defying Courts Makes Candidates Less Likely to Be Chosen: A Meta-Reanalysis of Conjoint Experiments**

This project examines whether respondents are less likely to choose hypothetical political candidates who defy judicial authority. Using replication files from 20 candidate conjoint experiments, I reconstruct direct comparisons between court-defying candidates and their opponents and estimate how the effect of a court-defying position on candidate choice differs depending on whether the defying candidate shares respondents' party affiliation or policy preferences. This project was my first-year paper at Northwestern.
